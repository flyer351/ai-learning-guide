Several AI products can now operate a web browser: reading pages, clicking buttons and typing into forms, while you watch. Examples include ChatGPT's agent mode, Claude's browser extension for Chrome, and AI features built into browsers such as Edge and Chrome. Names and availability change, and some require a paid plan.

## Good first tasks

- "Find the three cheapest round-trip flights from St. Louis to Phoenix the second week of March, nonstop only. Don't book anything."
- "Look up the return policy on these three store websites and summarize them in a table."
- "Find appointment times for a first-time passport application at post offices within 20 miles of my ZIP code."

Notice the pattern: **research and gather**, then **you** decide and act.

<div class="try">

**Try it now.** If your assistant has a browsing or agent mode, try a low-stakes research task:

> Visit the websites of three public libraries near [your town] and list their hours, any free classes for adults this month, and whether they lend out anything unusual (tools, telescopes, museum passes).

If it doesn't have an agent mode, ask the same question normally and see how its answer differs.

</div>

## Stay in the driver's seat

- **Watch the first few times.** Most tools show you each step. Stop it if it goes somewhere unexpected.
- **Don't let it buy things on its own** until you're very comfortable, and even then, set a limit.
- **Use a separate browser profile** for agent tasks, without your saved passwords, so it can't sign in to your bank by accident.
- **Expect it to be slow.** Agents are often slower than doing it yourself, the first time. The value is that you can walk away.

<div class="warn">

**Good to know.** Websites can contain hidden text written to trick AI agents into doing something you didn't ask for. This is called "prompt injection." Reputable tools defend against it, but it's another reason to keep agents away from your money and passwords. Lesson 5 covers this.

</div>
