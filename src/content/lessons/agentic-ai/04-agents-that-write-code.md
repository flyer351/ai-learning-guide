This is the change that makes the rest of this guide possible. **Coding agents** write, run and fix computer code from a plain-English description. People with no programming background are building working apps and websites with them.

## Examples

- **Claude Code** (Anthropic) and **Codex** (OpenAI) work in a computer's terminal, on the web, or in a desktop app.
- **Xcode**, Apple's free app-building software, can connect to AI assistants such as ChatGPT and Claude to write and change code for you.
- **Website builders** such as Replit, Lovable and Bolt let you describe a site and watch it appear.

Most work best on a paid plan, usually about $20 a month. You can start with free tiers in many cases.

## What working with one feels like

1. You describe what you want: "A simple app that tracks my daily walks and shows a weekly chart."
2. The agent makes a plan, writes the code, and tries running it.
3. You try the result and give feedback: "The chart is too small. Make the buttons bigger."
4. Repeat until you're happy.

You become the **product manager**: deciding what to build, testing it, and judging whether it's good. Your years of work experience matter here more than you might expect.

## What it can't do for you

- Decide what's worth building. That's your judgment.
- Guarantee the code is secure or bug-free. Simple personal projects are low-risk; anything handling other people's data or money needs more care.
- Skip the learning entirely. You'll pick up the basics along the way, and that's a good thing.
