Some AI apps for Mac and Windows can be given permission to open, rename, move and create files on your computer. That's handy, and it's also the one place in this guide where a mistake could affect files you care about. So this lesson starts with the safe way and keeps it simple.

## Start with a regular chat

You don't need to give an AI access to your computer to get help with your files. In an ordinary chat, in your browser or the phone app, you can **attach** files: photos, PDFs, spreadsheets. The AI works on a copy. Your originals never leave their folder, and nothing on your computer can change.

## Tasks that work well this way

- **Receipts to spreadsheet:** attach photos of 20 receipts and ask, "Make a spreadsheet with date, store, amount and category."
- **Paperwork check:** attach this year's tax documents and ask, "Make a checklist of what I have, and what's usually needed that I might be missing."
- **Family photos:** attach a few scans and ask, "Read the handwritten date on the back of each and suggest a file name like 1974-06-Lake-Trip.jpg." You rename them yourself.
- **Tidying up:** describe a messy folder and ask for a simple folder plan you can follow.

## If you try a desktop agent someday

When you're comfortable and a job is too big to do by hand, you may want an AI app that works on files directly. Follow three rules, every time.

1. **One copy folder, nothing else.** Make a new folder called `AI work`, **copy** (don't move) the files you want help with into it, and give the agent that one folder only. If something goes wrong, your originals are untouched.
2. **Back up first.** On a Mac, **Time Machine** is the built-in backup: plug in an external drive, turn it on in **System Settings › General › Time Machine**, and it quietly saves copies of your files every hour. Windows has the same idea, called **File History**. If you're not sure your backup is working, wait.
3. **Watch it work.** Ask it to show its plan before it changes anything, stay at your computer while it runs, and never let it delete. Have it move unwanted files into a `to delete` folder you empty yourself.

<div class="warn">

**Good to know.** Never point an agent at your whole Documents folder, your whole computer, or anything with banking or tax login details in it. If an app asks for more access than one folder, say no.

</div>

<div class="takeaway">

**The takeaway.** Attaching files to a chat covers most of what you'd want, with no risk to your computer. If you do try a desktop agent, give it one copy folder, have a backup, and watch it work.

</div>
