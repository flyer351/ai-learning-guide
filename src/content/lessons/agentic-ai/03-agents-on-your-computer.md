Desktop AI apps, such as the ChatGPT and Claude apps for Mac and Windows, can be given access to folders on your computer. Inside those folders they can read, sort, rename and create files. Microsoft is also building agent features into Windows and Office.

## Tasks that shine

- **Tidy up:** "Sort everything in my Downloads folder into subfolders by type, and list anything that looks like a duplicate."
- **Receipts to spreadsheet:** "Read the 40 receipt photos in this folder and make a spreadsheet with date, store, amount and category."
- **Family history:** "Rename these scanned photos using the handwritten date on the back, like 1974-06-Lake-Trip.jpg."
- **Paperwork:** "Read the PDFs in my 'Taxes 2026' folder and make a checklist of documents I have and what seems to be missing compared to last year."

## Sensible limits

1. **Give access to one folder, not your whole computer.** Make a working folder and copy files in.
2. **Keep a backup** before any big reorganization. Time Machine on a Mac or File History on Windows makes this automatic.
3. **Ask for a plan first:** "Before you change anything, tell me exactly what you'll do." Then approve it.
4. **Deleting should be your job.** Have it move things to a "to delete" folder instead.

<div class="try">

**Try it now.** Even without an agent, you can practice the planning step. Describe a messy folder to your assistant:

> My Pictures folder has about 3,000 photos from 2005 to today, all in one big folder with names like IMG_4581.jpg. Suggest a simple folder structure and a step-by-step plan to organize them.

</div>

<div class="takeaway">

**The takeaway.** Desktop agents are like a temp worker for your filing cabinet. Give them a clear task, a limited area to work in, and check the result.
