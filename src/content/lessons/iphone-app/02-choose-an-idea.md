The best first app solves a small problem **you** have. You'll know exactly what it should do, you'll be motivated to finish it, and you'll be its first happy user.

## Three tests for a first idea

1. **One sentence.** Can you describe it in one sentence? "An app that logs my blood pressure readings and shows a weekly chart."
2. **One to three screens.** A list, a detail page, a settings page. That's plenty.
3. **Works offline, on one phone.** No accounts, no servers, no sharing between users. This keeps it simple and private.

## Draw on your experience

Your career is full of app ideas. A former machinist might build a speeds-and-feeds calculator. An office manager might build a meeting-minutes template. A quilter might build a fabric-yardage calculator. Small niche tools are where hobbyist apps do best.

<div class="try">

**Try it now.** Brainstorm with your assistant:

> I'm a beginner building my first iPhone app with AI help. My background is [your career and hobbies]. Suggest 10 small app ideas that would fit on one to three screens, work offline, and need no user accounts. For each, give one sentence on who would use it.

Pick one. Then ask:

> Write a one-page plan for [your idea]: what each screen shows, what buttons it has, and what information it saves.

</div>

Save that plan. You'll paste it into the AI when you start building.

<div class="warn">

**Good to know.** Don't worry about whether your idea will make money. Make something you'll use. The [Money, honestly](../../money-honestly/) path explains realistic expectations if you're curious.
