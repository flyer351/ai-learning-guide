Yes, for a simple app. With an AI coding assistant doing most of the typing, people with no programming background now build and publish small, useful iPhone apps. It still takes patience: think of it as a hobby project over a few weeks, not an afternoon.

## What you'll need

<div class="table">

| Item | Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| A Mac | You may own one | Apple's app-building software, **Xcode**, only runs on macOS. A Mac from the last 4 or 5 years with 16 GB of memory is comfortable. |
| An iPhone | You likely own one | For testing your app on a real phone. |
| Xcode | Free | Download from the Mac App Store. |
| An AI assistant | Free to about $20/month | A paid plan helps for longer coding sessions. |
| Apple Developer Program | $99/year | **Only when you're ready to publish.** You can build and test on your own phone without it. |

</div>

## What about Windows?

You can't build iPhone apps directly on Windows. If you have a Windows PC, the [website path](../../website/) is a great project instead, and everything you learn about working with AI carries over.

## What kind of app is realistic?

**Realistic first apps:** a habit tracker, a medication reminder log, a recipe box, a tip or unit calculator for your old trade, a quiz for grandkids, a checklist for a hobby.

**Not first apps:** anything with user accounts, payments, chat between users, or handling other people's private data. Those need more skill to do safely.

## What you'll learn along the way

You'll pick up the vocabulary: **Swift** (Apple's programming language), **SwiftUI** (the way screens are built), **simulator** (a pretend iPhone on your Mac screen). You won't need to memorize them. The AI handles the details, and you learn by watching.

<div class="takeaway">

**The takeaway.** A Mac, an iPhone, free software and some patience. The $99 fee waits until you have something worth publishing.

</div>
