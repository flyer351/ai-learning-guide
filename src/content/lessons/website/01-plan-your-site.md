A website is one of the friendliest first projects. It costs nothing to publish, it works on any computer, and mistakes are easy to fix.

## What could your site be?

- **A hobby site:** your woodworking projects, garden journal or classic car restoration.
- **Family history:** stories, old photos and a family tree for the grandkids.
- **A small-business page:** hours, services and contact details for a side business or a friend's shop.
- **A resource you know well:** a guide to local fishing spots, tips from your trade, a list of resources for caregivers.
- **A support page for your app:** the App Store requires a privacy policy page, and this is an easy way to host one.

## Keep version one small

Most good first sites have three to five pages:

1. **Home:** who you are and what the site is about
2. **One to three content pages:** projects, stories, services or guides
3. **About or Contact:** a little about you, and how to reach you (an email address is enough)

> I'm planning my first simple website about [topic]. It's for [who will read it]. Suggest a list of 3 to 5 pages, what goes on each, and a short, friendly name for the site.

## Static is simple

The kind of site in this path is called a **static site**: a set of pages that look the same for every visitor. No logins, no databases, no shopping carts. That makes it free to host, fast, and very hard to hack. This guide you're reading is a static site.

<div class="takeaway">

**The takeaway.** Pick a topic you care about and keep it to a handful of pages. You can always add more later.

</div>
