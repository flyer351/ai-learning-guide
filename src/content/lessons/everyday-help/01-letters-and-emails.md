Writing is where most people first fall for AI. A letter that might take 30 minutes of staring at a blank page takes two minutes to draft and five to polish.

## Three ways to use it

1. **Draft from scratch.** Tell it the situation and what you want to happen.
2. **Polish your own draft.** Paste in what you wrote and ask it to tighten, soften or correct it.
3. **Help with a reply.** Paste the email you received and say how you'd like to respond.

<div class="try">

**Try it now.** Adapt this for a real situation:

> Help me write a short email to my insurance company. My claim for a cracked windshield (claim #12345) was approved on May 2, but I haven't received payment. Ask politely when I can expect it. Sign it "Pat Miller."

</div>

## Keep it sounding like you

AI writing can sound stiff or over-eager ("I hope this email finds you well!"). Fix that by telling it how you write:

- "Write it the way a straightforward retired plant manager would. No fluff."
- "Make it warmer. This is to my cousin."
- Paste a couple of emails you've written and say: "Match this style."

## A useful trick for difficult messages

When you're upset, write the angry version first, then ask:

> Rewrite this so it's firm and clear but professional. Keep every fact. Remove anything that sounds sarcastic.

You get to say what you mean without burning a bridge.

<div class="warn">

**Good to know.** Always read the final version before sending. You're the one signing it. And replace any personal account numbers after you paste the AI's draft into your email, rather than typing them into the AI.

</div>
