You've balanced books and managed budgets for years. AI won't replace that judgment, but it's a fast helper for the setup and the arithmetic.

## Build a budget

Tell it your categories and rough amounts, and ask for a table. Then refine it.

<div class="try">

**Try it now.** Change the numbers to fit you:

> Help me build a simple monthly retirement budget. Income: Social Security $2,400, pension $900. Expenses: mortgage $1,100, utilities about $250, groceries $500, car and gas $300, insurance $280, phone and internet $120, gifts and fun $200. Show it as a table with totals and what's left over. Then suggest three areas I could look at if I wanted to save $150 a month.

</div>

## Make a spreadsheet without learning formulas

Ask: "Turn this into a spreadsheet I can paste into Excel or Google Sheets, with formulas for the totals." Many assistants can also create a downloadable spreadsheet file directly.

## Compare options

- "Compare paying off my car loan early versus keeping the money in a CD at 4%. Loan balance $8,000 at 6.5%, 30 months left."
- "Is a 15-year or 30-year mortgage better if I'm 64? Show the trade-offs, not a recommendation."

## Make sense of your spending

You can download transactions from your bank as a spreadsheet (often called a CSV file), remove the account number, and upload it with: "Group these by category and show the monthly totals."

<div class="warn">

**Good to know.** Double-check any calculation that a decision depends on. Ask "Show your math," and redo the key numbers with a calculator. AI is a tool for thinking, not a licensed financial advisor.

</div>
