AI is a useful partner for understanding your health. It's not a doctor, and good assistants will tell you so. Used well, it helps you get more out of the time with your real doctor.

## Before the appointment

<div class="try">

**Try it now.**

> I have a follow-up appointment about my high blood pressure. I've been on lisinopril for three months and I sometimes feel dizzy when I stand up. Help me prepare a short list of questions to ask my doctor, ordered by importance.

</div>

Print the list or keep it on your phone.

## After the appointment

- "Explain what an A1C of 6.1 means, in plain English."
- "My doctor mentioned 'mild degenerative disc disease.' What does that usually involve?"
- "What lifestyle changes are commonly recommended for this?"

You can photograph lab results and ask for an explanation. Crop out your name, birth date and patient ID first.

## What AI should not do

- Replace your doctor's advice, or change your medication.
- Diagnose something urgent. **For chest pain, stroke signs or other emergencies, call 911.**
- Decide between treatments for you. It can help you understand the choices.

## Medicare and insurance

AI can explain the difference between Medicare Advantage and Medigap, what Part D covers, or how an appeal works. Rules and plans change every year, so confirm details at [Medicare.gov](https://www.medicare.gov) or with your [State Health Insurance Assistance Program (SHIP)](https://www.shiphelp.org), which offers free, unbiased counseling.

<div class="warn">

**Good to know.** Check medication questions with your pharmacist. They're free, they know your prescriptions, and they're often easier to reach than your doctor.

</div>
