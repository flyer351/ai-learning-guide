You've probably heard "AI" used for everything from self-driving cars to the spell-checker on your phone. In this guide, AI mostly means one thing: **AI assistants**, such as ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini and Copilot. You type to them in everyday language, and they type back.

## How it works, in one paragraph

An AI assistant has read an enormous amount of text: books, websites, manuals, articles. From all that reading, it learned the patterns of language well enough to write sensible replies to almost anything. It doesn't look answers up in a filing cabinet. It writes each answer fresh, word by word, based on those patterns. That's why it can draft a letter in your voice, and also why it can occasionally state something false with total confidence.

## What it's good at

- **Writing and rewriting.** Letters, emails, summaries, toasts, complaint letters, product descriptions.
- **Explaining.** Medicare terms, a confusing bill, how a heat pump works, what a word means in context.
- **Organizing.** Turning a messy list into a plan, a budget, a packing list or a schedule.
- **Brainstorming.** Gift ideas, names for a project, questions to ask a contractor.
- **Writing computer code.** This is how people with no programming background now build apps and websites.

## What it's not

- **Not a person.** It has no feelings or memories of you, beyond what you've told it in the conversation (some tools offer an optional memory feature you can turn off).
- **Not always right.** It can invent facts, book titles or quotes. This is called a "hallucination."
- **Not a search engine,** though many assistants can now search the web when they need to.
- **Not magic money.** Anyone selling AI as a way to get rich quickly is selling something else. More on that in [Money, honestly](../../money-honestly/).

<div class="takeaway">

**The takeaway.** Think of an AI assistant as a very well-read, very fast, eager new assistant. Brilliant at first drafts and explanations. Needs supervision on facts.
