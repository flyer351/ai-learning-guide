AI assistants are run by companies, and what you type is stored on their computers. Some companies may use conversations to improve future versions of their AI. A few sensible habits keep you safe.

## Never type these into an AI

- Passwords or PINs
- Social Security, Medicare or driver's license numbers
- Bank, credit card or brokerage account numbers
- Anything you wouldn't want a stranger at the company to read

## Fine to share, with judgment

- General health questions ("What are common side effects of statins?"), without your full name and date of birth
- Your situation in general terms ("I'm 67, retired, with a small pension")
- Documents with personal details blacked out or removed first

## Settings worth a look

Each assistant has a settings or privacy page. Look for options like these (names vary):

1. **"Improve the model for everyone"** or **"Help improve"**: turn this off if you don't want your chats used for training.
2. **Memory**: some assistants remember details across conversations. Handy, but you can view, delete or turn it off.
3. **Chat history**: you can delete individual conversations or all of them.

<div class="try">

**Try it now.** Ask the assistant itself:

> Where are your privacy settings, and how do I stop my conversations from being used to train your AI? Give me step-by-step directions.

Then follow the steps. If the directions don't match what you see, tell it so. Menus change often.

</div>

## Watch out for look-alikes

Scammers make fake AI apps and websites with names like "ChatGPT Pro Plus AI." Download apps only from the official App Store or Google Play, and check that the maker is OpenAI, Anthropic, Google or Microsoft.
