Every AI assistant makes mistakes. Knowing how and when they happen is what separates a confident user from a frustrated one.

## The most common mistakes

- **Made-up facts.** Invented statistics, book titles, court cases or quotes. They look completely real.
- **Out-of-date information.** Each AI learned from text up to a certain date. Unless it searches the web, it may not know about recent changes to prices, laws or products.
- **Math slips.** Better than it used to be, but still worth checking on anything important.
- **Agreeing too easily.** If you push back, some assistants will change a correct answer to a wrong one just to please you.

## How to check

1. **Ask for sources.** "Where does that come from? Give me links." Then click the links. If a link doesn't exist or says something different, be suspicious.
2. **Ask it to double-check itself.** "Review your answer for mistakes." It often catches its own errors.
3. **Cross-check with a second assistant.** Paste the answer into a different AI and ask, "Is this accurate?"
4. **Go to the source** for anything that matters: Medicare.gov, IRS.gov, your doctor, your accountant, the manufacturer's manual.

<div class="try">

**Try it now.** Ask your assistant something you already know the answer to well, from your career or hobby. For example:

> What are the most common causes of scrap in a plastic injection molding line, and how do you reduce each one?

See how it does. Where is it right? Where is it vague or wrong? You'll get a feel for its strengths.

</div>

## A simple rule of thumb

<div class="takeaway">

**Low stakes, trust and enjoy.** Recipes, gift ideas, letters you'll read before sending.

**High stakes, verify.** Health, medication, money, taxes, legal questions, anything you'd regret getting wrong.

</div>
