Websites can earn money in a few ways. All of them depend on one thing: **a lot of visitors**, built up slowly over time with genuinely useful content.

## The main ways

- **Display ads** (for example, Google AdSense). Ads appear on your pages and you earn a share. Small sites commonly earn somewhere from a few dollars to around $20 per 1,000 page views, depending on the topic. Approval requires a site with real, original content.
- **Affiliate links.** You recommend a product and earn a small commission when someone buys through your link. You must clearly disclose these links to readers; the Federal Trade Commission requires it.
- **Selling something of your own:** a printed guide, a pattern, a service, or your app.

## A worked example

Say your woodworking site grows to 300 visitors a day (that's a lot for a hobby site, and often takes a year or more). Each views 2 pages, so 18,000 page views a month. At $8 per 1,000 views, that's about **$144 a month** from ads.

Real, and nice. Also the result of steady work over a long time.

## The trade-offs of ads

Ads bring tracking cookies, slower pages, and consent banners. Some readers find them off-putting. Many hobby sites choose to stay ad-free, as this guide does, and some add ads only once they have a large audience.
