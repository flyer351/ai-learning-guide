Some people earn a modest side income from small apps. Most apps earn little or nothing. Both are true, and it's worth knowing which to expect before you spend money chasing the first.

## The honest picture

- The App Store has well over a million apps, and thousands of new ones arrive every week. AI tools have made building apps easier, which means far more competition.
- Downloads are concentrated in a small number of big, well-known apps. A typical hobby app gets a handful of downloads a day, if that.
- Apps that do earn steady money usually serve a **specific niche well** (a tool for a particular trade, hobby or condition), get **updated regularly**, and are found through **word of mouth** in that community.

## How apps make money

<div class="table">

| Model | How it works | Reality for a hobby app |
|---|---|---|
| Paid | Pay once to download, e.g. $2.99 | Hard: people rarely pay up front for unknown apps |
| Free with upgrade | Basic features free, extras unlocked with a one-time purchase | The most common model for small developers |
| Subscription | Monthly or yearly fee | Needs ongoing value, such as new content or syncing |
| Ads | Free app shows ads | Pennies per user; needs very large numbers of users |

</div>

Apple keeps a commission on sales (15% for most small developers in the Small Business Program, at the time of writing).

## Do the math before you spend

A $2.99 upgrade earns you about $2.54 after Apple's 15%. To cover just the **$99 yearly developer fee**, you'd need about 39 upgrades a year. To earn $100 a month, about 40 a month.

That's achievable for a good niche app. It's a long way from "replace your income."

<div class="try">

**Try it now.**

> I'm thinking about building an iPhone app for [niche]. Help me estimate realistically how many people might want it, how I could reach them for free, and what a reasonable price would be. Be candid if the market looks very small.

</div>

<div class="takeaway">

**The takeaway.** Build apps because you enjoy it and they help someone. Treat any income as a pleasant bonus, and never spend more chasing it than you'd spend on any other hobby.

</div>
