Ads promising "passive income with AI apps" are everywhere, and many are aimed at retirees. Some programs are outright scams. Many more are **upsell funnels**: a modest first purchase that leads, step by step, to much larger ones. They often include some real teaching, which makes them harder to judge.

None of this means you'd be foolish to be interested. These programs are designed by professionals to be persuasive. The best protection is knowing the pattern.

## Warning signs

**In the ads and sales pages**

- Income promises: "first Apple payment in weeks," "replace your pension"
- "No experience needed" combined with "10 minutes a day"
- Countdown timers, "only 3 spots left," or "price goes up tonight"
- Testimonials from people like you, with large earnings and no way to verify them
- Live counters like "1,200 people joined this week"

**In the fine print**

- Refund windows of just a few days, or guarantees with conditions you can't see before paying
- Required arbitration and no class actions, which limits your options if things go wrong
- A company address that doesn't check out (search it on a map), or ownership that's hard to find or overseas

**After you buy**

- A ladder of upsells: a few hundred dollars, then a few thousand, then "elite" programs costing $10,000 or more
- Partner companies for LLCs, taxes, business credit or trusts, presented as necessary next steps
- Offers to finance the next purchase, or to "build business credit" first
- A friendly "advisor" or "success coach" whose calls end with a sales pitch
- Pressure to decide on the call

## Before you pay for any AI program

1. **Wait 48 hours.** Real opportunities survive a weekend.
2. **Search** the company name plus "complaints," "refund" and "BBB."
3. **Read the refund policy and terms** before paying. If you can't find them, don't buy.
4. **Ask a trusted person** to look it over with you.
5. **Pay with a credit card,** not a debit card, wire, gift card or crypto. Credit cards give you dispute rights.

## If you've already paid

You're not alone, and acting quickly helps.

1. **Stop further payments.** Don't sign new agreements, apply for financing, or move money while you think.
2. **Request cancellation and a refund in writing,** by email, even if the window has passed. Keep copies.
3. **Note any deadlines in your contract,** such as cancellation windows or arbitration opt-outs (some must be sent by certified mail).
4. **Call your credit card company** about a dispute if the product wasn't as promised.
5. **Get independent advice** before signing anything new, especially legal documents such as trusts: a local attorney or accountant with no ties to the program.
6. **Report it** to the FTC at [ReportFraud.ftc.gov](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov) and to your state attorney general. Reports help stop these programs for others.

If you'd like to talk it through with a person, the free [AARP Fraud Watch Network Helpline](https://www.aarp.org/fraudhelpline) (877-908-3360) is open to anyone, member or not.
