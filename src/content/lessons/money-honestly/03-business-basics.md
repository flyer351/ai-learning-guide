You've likely run a department, a household budget or even a business. You already know more about this than most app "coaches" assume. Here are the basics as they apply to a small app or website.

## You don't need an LLC to start

- You can publish an app as an **individual**, under your own name.
- You can run a website and earn ad income as an individual.
- In the US, money you earn this way is reported on your tax return. If you treat it as a business, that's typically a **sole proprietorship** reported on Schedule C. A tax professional can tell you what fits your situation.

An LLC can make sense later, for liability protection or once income is meaningful. It's rarely needed **before** you've earned anything.

## Three tax points worth knowing

These are general US rules. Your accountant can tell you how they apply to you.

- **Self-employment tax.** If you treat it as a business and your net profit is **$400 or more** in a year, you also file Schedule SE and pay self-employment tax (about 15.3%, for Social Security and Medicare), on top of income tax.
- **Hobby or business.** If it's a hobby rather than a business, the income is still reported, but hobby expenses (like the $99 Apple fee) can't be deducted. That's one real reason the question matters.
- **Social Security before full retirement age.** If you're collecting benefits and haven't reached full retirement age, earnings above an annual limit can temporarily reduce your checks. A small hobby income is usually well under it, but it's worth asking.

## Costs that are real

- Apple Developer Program: $99/year (only if you publish an app)
- A domain name: $10 to $20/year (optional)
- An AI assistant: free to about $20/month
- Your time

## Costs to question

Be cautious when a course or "academy" recommends, or requires, that you buy these **from its partners** before your app has earned anything:

- LLC formation packages
- Tax preparation or "tax strategy" subscriptions
- "Business credit" building
- Trusts or estate plans tied to the business
- Ongoing coaching or "mentorship" programs

Each might be legitimate on its own. Bought together, under time pressure, for a business with no income yet, they can add up to thousands of dollars.
